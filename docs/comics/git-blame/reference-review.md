## Extracted characteristics

**Line:** thin but definite dark outlines with human irregularity; small descriptive marks indicate folds, hair, bark and fur. Avoid blurry contours or mechanical vector uniformity.

**Color:** bounded local colors with subtly mottled wash/shading. Greens, ochres and warm browns dominate the environment; red, yellow, blue and purple garments help identify recurring figures. This is not a uniformly muted beige palette. Photograph lighting and printed reproduction affect apparent color, so do not infer exact paint pigments or medium.

**People:** simplified, expressive faces with strong noses, brows, hair shapes and readable hands/gestures. Some exaggeration is present, but bodies remain recognizable as human adults and children. For our adult cast, use slightly subtler expressions and natural adult proportions, without photorealistic portrait rendering.

**Perspective:** elevated oblique views reveal paths and several adjacent spaces. Perspective is illustrative and gently elastic, not a rigid isometric CAD scene. Adapt this to occasional office overview illustrations; use closer views when dialogue or emotion needs focus.

**Narrative:** recurring figures and props carry small actions across spreads. The pleasure comes from noticing cause, consequence and interactions. Our comic should preserve that observational quality with one or two supporting details, while keeping the main six-scene narrative clear on phones.

**Texture:** controlled texture inside readable shapes. The wood table, page gutter, wear, reflections and camera shadows belong to the photographed book, not to the target illustration treatment.
