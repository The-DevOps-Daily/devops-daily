# Chapter 1 — visual pacing revision

9 October 2026 · Proposal following user feedback · **Awaiting revised composition approval.**

The first chapter draft is technically sound and readable, but repeats medium views of engineers at desks. Changing expressions alone does not create enough rhythm. Preserve the approved character identities, warm ink/wash treatment, engineering mechanism and short dialogue; vary framing, physical action, environment and visual emphasis.

This proposal reopens Chapter 1’s visual review before Phase 6. Existing masters, prompts, screenshots and provenance remain intact. No replacement artwork has been generated for this proposal.

## Proposed sequence

| Scene                      | New visual beat                                                                                                                                            | Story purpose and production requirements                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Two lines               | Keep the approved warm establishing shot of Sam and Maya.                                                                                                  | Establish the office and their relationship. This is the one comfortable desk conversation we earn.                                                                                                                                                              |
| 2. Half a deployment       | Close view of Sam’s hand at the release control, with his chair turning as he prepares to leave; Alex appears at the edge of the composition with his mug. | Confidence becomes movement. Green replacement status is an authored overlay, not generated UI lettering or a claim that the entire rollout is safe. Reserve quiet space for the two lines.                                                                      |
| 3. Customers disagree      | Wider doorway view: Chris arrives with the customer report as the team starts packing up; chairs turn and Maya comes back to investigate.                  | Interrupt an action the reader understands. The team responds deliberately, without slapstick incompetence or exaggerated catastrophe. Establish doorway geography from the same office reference.                                                               |
| 4. We hung up mid-sentence | Elevated view at the whiteboard: Maya draws the path, Sam identifies the old target, Alex joins the dots. Their poses and eye lines follow the evidence.   | Move the investigation off the desk. Generate blank board space and add the controlled request/response diagram afterward; preserve separate requests, direct ALB IP-target routing and the interrupted old response. Mobile explanation remains in normal flow. |
| 5. The old handler         | Tight over-the-shoulder view of paired work: Sam at the code, Alex pointing out his existing handler, Maya preparing the test at the side.                 | Hands, attention and ownership carry the scene. Any necessary code/log fragments are authored overlays. Codex/Claude Code stay ordinary supervised tools. Avoid another frontal three-person laptop row.                                                         |
| 6. Boring graphs           | A quiet close reaction: Alex pauses mid-sip as the bounded slow request completes; Sam leans into frame. The visual emphasis is waiting, then relief.      | Contrast the earlier urgency with stillness. A small controlled response trace can establish completion without a dashboard wall. Keep the test-environment/observed-production caption and avoid an absolute zero-downtime claim.                               |
| 7. Monday                  | Return to the doorway: the team has bags and closed laptops; Chris makes the tiny-change gesture, catches their look and joins them leaving.               | An exit, a pause and “Monday” form the payoff. Preserve the manager’s good judgment. Do not add an incident souvenir mug that could not yet have been made.                                                                                                      |

The chapter alternates establishing view → close detail → wide interruption → investigation → close collaboration → quiet reaction → exit. Images should show something changing even with the dialogue hidden. Do not give every scene the same upper-third empty wall, portrait crop and seated eye level. Reserve dialogue space deliberately for each composition and recheck bubble tails after the new art exists.
