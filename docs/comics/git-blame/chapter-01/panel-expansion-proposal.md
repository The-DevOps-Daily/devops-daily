A panel is one visual moment; a scene can contain several panels. Traditional comic pages often use around four to six panels, with substantial variation. Scrolling comics have no universal chapter count or fixed page grid. For this short incident, use fourteen panels across the existing story beats, rather than treating seven large illustrations as a fixed limit. This is a pacing choice, not an industry rule.

## Art direction changes

- Alternate the office, a customer's environment, tight hands/objects, evidence and wider action. Keep the full character sheets as identity references, but do not place every face in every panel.
- Technical details must carry a story consequence: a pending purchase, the selected old target, the abrupt-exit line, the request finishing, the handoff completing. Avoid decorative Kubernetes symbols, floating cloud logos and unreadable pseudo-code.
- Use varied daylight and screen light within the same Friday, cooler slate accents in evidence views, and warm office colors in human moments. The original reference review calls for lively local color, not a uniform amber/beige wash.
- Preserve fine irregular ink and mottled washes; avoid uniform thick animation contours, polished gradients, identical smiles and excessively smooth surfaces. Keep adult faces and approved clothing recognizable.
- Give technical inserts landscape or square framing, with detailed foregrounds. Human scenes can remain portrait. An image does not need a large blank wall when it has no speech bubbles.
- Let expressions follow the incident: optimism, interrupted plans, concentrated investigation, ownership, waiting, relief. Competent engineers can be concerned without panic or incompetence.
- Keep illustrated diagrams in the generated art, with exactly reviewed topology. Keep code, status labels and dialogue separately authored. Phone and enlarged-text layouts must expose their full accessible meaning in normal flow.
- Preserve all current source masters and provenance. “Provisional reuse” means a candidate, not a declaration that the user approved these illustrations as final.

### Evidence caveat

The trace panel is a stitched investigation view, not a claim that Kubernetes emits per-request SIGTERM records or that an ALB supplies application shutdown logs. Match the ALB trace field to the application's logged `X-Amzn-Trace-Id`, the old target address and process-lifetime evidence. `r-17` is a short display alias for the fictional matched trace, not a literal AWS trace ID. A stop event is process-level; the known handler and the observed request/exit ordering explain it. If the final artwork cannot communicate those distinctions accurately, simplify the labels rather than invent telemetry.
